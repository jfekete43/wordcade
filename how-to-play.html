How To Play

Lexathon is a word game built around one familiar idea — guess the hidden five-letter word — wrapped in four very different ways to play. Every mode shares the same core: type a five-letter word, press Enter, and the tiles tell you how close you were.

The Basics

You get five guesses per word. After each guess, every letter is colour-coded:

  • Cyan — right letter, right position.
  • Magenta — that letter is in the word, but somewhere else.
  • Grey — that letter isn't in the word at all.

Guesses must be real words from the dictionary. Solving faster is worth dramatically more:

That curve is steep on purpose. A first-guess solve is worth fifty times a fifth-guess one, so the difference between good players isn't whether they solve words — it's how early.

Colour-blind players can swap the cyan/magenta pair for a blue/orange one under Menu → Settings → Colourblind Mode.

Standard — The Endless Run

One word after another, for as long as you can keep going. There's no timer and no round limit; a run ends when you decide it ends, or when you lose it.

Continues

You start each run with one Continue. Miss a word while you still have it, and you spend the Continue and carry on with your score intact. Miss a second word with no Continue left and the run is over — and you lose everything you'd built up in it.

Cashing Out

This is the decision the whole mode turns on. Cash Out at any time to bank your score permanently: it's added to your Wallet and your Career Bank, and it can never be taken away. Keep playing and every word is another chance to add 500 points — or to lose the lot.

There's no correct answer, which is the point. A 4,000-point run you cashed out beats a 12,000-point run you didn't.

Clash — Head-To-Head

Two players, seven minutes, the same list of words in the same order. Scoring matches Standard, with one change: missing a word costs 50 points instead of ending your run. You're never knocked out, so the pressure is about pace rather than survival.

Winning

  • Open up a 1,000-point lead and the match ends immediately.
  • Otherwise whoever is ahead when the clock hits zero takes it.
  • A win pays 1,000 points into your Wallet and Career Bank.

Skips

You get two skips per match. A skip abandons the current word and moves you to the next one with no penalty — no points gained, but no 50 lost either. They exist so a word you simply cannot see doesn't quietly drain the clock while your opponent pulls away. Spend them deliberately: two is not many across seven minutes.

Sudden Death

Level at the buzzer and the match goes to Sudden Death: one shared secret word, five guesses each, first to solve it wins. If neither of you gets it, the match is recorded as a genuine tie.

Ranked And Unranked

Matches from the public queue move your MMR. Private rooms — the ones you create with a room code or an invite link — don't, so you can play friends without risking your rank. Private rooms also offer a one-tap Share Invite link and a Rematch button.

Leaving a match mid-game forfeits it and your opponent is awarded the win.

Gauntlet — The Daily Puzzle

One puzzle a day, identical for everyone: ten words, one attempt. It resets at midnight Eastern.

Scoring is the same 500/250/150/50/10, but there are no Continues. Miss a single word and the run stops there, keeping whatever you'd scored to that point. Getting all ten is genuinely hard.

Afterwards you can share your result as a spoiler-free grid — one digit per word showing how many guesses it took, or ❌ for a miss — plus your score and your average guesses. The Gauntlet leaderboard only ever shows today's attempts, so it resets alongside the puzzle.

FFA — Free-For-All

The same seven-minute race as Clash, opened up to three to six players at once. You also get two skips here.

There's no early finish — no 1,000-point knockout. It runs the full clock and the highest score wins. Payouts scale with where you place rather than being all-or-nothing:

Points, Shop And Challenges

Wallet vs Career Bank

Two separate numbers. Your Wallet is spendable currency for the Shop. Your Career Bank is your lifetime total and is never reduced by spending — it's what the All Time leaderboard ranks you on. Buying cosmetics costs you nothing in standing.

The Shop

Titles, banners and name effects. All of it is cosmetic — none of it affects scoring, difficulty, or matchmaking. A few items are seasonal and only purchasable inside their window, but once bought they're yours permanently.

Challenges

Three Daily challenges rotate every day, and Career challenges are one-time milestones. Both pay points into your Wallet, and both are claimed manually from the Challenges menu — nothing expires unclaimed mid-session, but dailies do reset.

Accounts

You can play immediately without signing up. The catch is that a guest profile lives only in that one browser: clear your data or switch device and it's gone, with no way to recover it. Linking a Google account or an email address keeps everything — wallet, rank, cosmetics, stats — and lets you play from anywhere. Linking preserves the progress you've already made; it doesn't start you over.
