About Lexathon

Lexathon is a competitive word game. You guess five-letter words, five attempts each — but instead of one puzzle a day and a wait until tomorrow, there's a scoring system, ranked multiplayer, leaderboards and a cosmetic shop built around it.

Why It Exists

Daily word puzzles are excellent and take about four minutes. Then they're done. The pattern-solving they're built on is satisfying enough to want more of, and there wasn't an obvious place to go for it — somewhere you could play twenty words in a row, or race someone else through the same list, or find out whether you're actually good at this or just enjoy it.

So Lexathon keeps the familiar guessing loop and asks different questions around it: how long do you keep a run going before banking it? Can you solve faster than the person across from you? Where do you sit against everyone else?

What Makes It Different

Three things, mainly.

Solving fast is what scores. A first-guess solve is worth 500 points and a fifth-guess solve is worth 10. Nearly everyone gets there eventually; the scoring only cares how quickly. That single curve is what turns a puzzle into a game with a skill ceiling.

You choose when to stop. In the endless Standard mode, points aren't yours until you Cash Out — and a run that goes wrong takes everything with it. Knowing when to walk away turns out to be as much of a skill as the words.

It's genuinely multiplayer. Clash puts two players on the same word list for seven minutes. Free-For-All does it with up to four. Both are real-time against real opponents, with a shared MMR rating and a rank that moves when you win or lose.

How It's Built

Lexathon runs in the browser with nothing to install, on phones and desktops alike. Accounts are optional — you can play instantly as a guest, and link a Google account or email later if you want your progress to survive switching device.

Scores, wallets, ranks and match results are all validated server-side rather than trusted from the browser, which matters when leaderboards mean something. The daily Gauntlet's answers never reach your device until you've guessed, so the puzzle can't be read ahead of time.

Points And Cosmetics

Points earned in any mode go to two places: a Wallet you can spend, and a Career Bank that only ever goes up and is what the All Time leaderboard ranks you by. Spending never costs you standing.

The shop sells titles, banners and name effects. All of it is cosmetic — there is nothing to buy that makes you score more, match easier opponents, or see more information than anyone else. There are no real-money purchases: points have no cash value, can't be bought, and can't be exchanged for anything outside the game.

The Modes

Standard — endless solo runs, one Continue, cash out whenever.
Clash — 1v1, seven minutes, first to a 1,000-point lead or ahead at the buzzer.
Gauntlet — one shared daily puzzle, ten words, no second chances.
FFA — up to four players racing at once, payouts by placement.

Full rules for all four are in How To Play.

Still Being Built

Lexathon is actively developed and changes regularly — modes get added, balance gets adjusted, and bugs get fixed as players report them. If something looks broken or unfair, it's worth saying so; most of what's been fixed recently came from players noticing.
